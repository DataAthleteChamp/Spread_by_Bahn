Idea 1 · price for contribution

Find the price/volume trade-off for each request.

Choose the quote that maximises
P(win | quote, current state) × expected contribution.

Expected contribution accounts for driver fees, delivery risk and the value of dealer trust. An online model updates from our own wins and losses.

Lane effects provide a starting point; online updates respond when this week's market differs. No competitor prices are available to us.

More orders are valuable only if their contribution survives recruitment, refunds and the qualification constraint.

Idea 2 · recruit with a time-dependent policy

Optimise the fee schedule, not just the opening fee.

A booking-hazard model and a drop model feed a finite-horizon dynamic program. It weighs waiting, recruitment cost, deadline risk and drivers learning to expect raises.

Idea 3 · respect the real delivery promise

Readiness, notes and deadlines are economic inputs.

Finnish, Swedish and English notes can change pickup readiness, site hours, driver requirements or delivery deadlines. A nominally safe timestamp is not enough.

We added note handling, charging rules, delayed posting and a projected on-time controller. The safety net favours low-risk wins when the projected delivery share deteriorates.

Not a guarantee: FINAL still fails the known stress seed c17-52 at 89.90% on time. Correlated shortages remain a real risk.
